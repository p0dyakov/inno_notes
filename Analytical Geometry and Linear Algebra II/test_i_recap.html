    Problem 4: Statement

    Why are the following statements false?

    • If V is orthogonal to W, then the orthogonal complement of V is orthogonal to the orthogonal complement of W
    • If V is orthogonal to W, and W is orthogonal to Z, then the orthogonal complement of V is orthogonal to Z

    109

    Slide 110

    Problem 4: Solution (1/2)

    Th. 1. If V is orthogonal to W, then the orthogonal complement of V is orthogonal to the orthogonal complement of W.

    Assume that \(V, W \subset \mathbb{R}^3, V = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right\}, W = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right\}\).

    That is why: \(V^{\perp} = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}, W^{\perp} = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}\)

    \(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{w}\) should be zero by definition of orthogonality for any vectors v, w. However, taking \(\mathbf{v} = [0 \ 0 \ 1]^T\) and \(\mathbf{w} = [0 \ 0 \ 1]^T\), \(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{w} = 1\). Contradiction!

    110

    Slide 111

    Problem 4: Solution (2/2)

    Th. 2. If V is orthogonal to W, and W is orthogonal to Z, then the orthogonal complement of V is orthogonal to Z.

    Assume that \(V, W, Z \subset \mathbb{R}^3, V = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right\}, W = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right\}, Z = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}\).

    Then, \(V^{\perp} = \text{span} \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}\).

    \(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{z}\) should be zero by definition of orthogonality for any vectors v, z. However, taking \(\mathbf{v} = [0 \ 0 \ 1]^T\) and \(\mathbf{z} = [1 \ 0 \ 1]^T\), \(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{z} = 1\). Contradiction!
