    Proof by Induction We will use induction on the size \(n\) of the matrix.

    Base Case (\(n = 1\)) For \(n = 1\), \(A =\), and its inverse is \(A^{-1} =\), which is trivially upper triangular.

    Inductive Hypothesis Assume that for an \((n - 1) \times (n - 1)\) upper triangular matrix, its inverse is also upper triangular.

    Inductive Step Consider an \(n \times n\) upper triangular matrix \(A\). Partition \(A\) as: \[A = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \\ 0 & A_{22} \end{bmatrix} ,\] where: * \(A_{11}\) is an \((n - 1) \times (n - 1)\) upper triangular matrix, * \(A_{12}\) is an \((n - 1) \times 1\) matrix, * \(A_{22}\) is a \(1 \times 1\) matrix (a scalar).

    Since \(A\) is invertible, both \(A_{11}\) and \(A_{22}\) are invertible (because the determinant of \(A\) is the product of the determinants of \(A_{11}\) and \(A_{22}\), and \(\det(A) \neq 0\)).

    Image Description This page is purely printed text detailing the beginning of a mathematical proof by induction.

    Slide 10 (Page 10 in PDF)

    The inverse of \(A\) can be written as: \[A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} B_{11} & B_{12} \\ 0 & B_{22} \end{bmatrix} , \quad A = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \\ 0 & A_{22} \end{bmatrix}\]

    where: * \(B_{11} = A_{11}^{-1}\), * \(B_{12} = -A_{11}^{-1}A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}\), * \(B_{22} = A_{22}^{-1}\).

    By the inductive hypothesis, \(A_{11}^{-1}\) is upper triangular. Also, \(B_{12}\) involves products of upper triangular matrices and scalars, so it is upper triangular. Finally, \(B_{22}\) is a scalar, which is trivially upper triangular.

    Thus, \(A^{-1}\) is upper triangular.
